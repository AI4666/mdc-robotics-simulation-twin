1.1 Interconnected Operational Processes (Lifecycle from Request to Delivery)

Rather than a simplistic single queue-and-service model, Option 2 models seven interconnected operational subsystems that interact stochastically:

  1. Request Generation Subsystem: Dynamic arrival rates driven by campus schedule phases (morning administrative mail bursts, midday student lab sample transfers, afternoon urgent department dispatches). Models Poisson arrivals with time-dependent arrival rates λ(t).
  2. Priority & Service Level Agreement (SLA) Stratification: Requests are segregated into three tiers:
    • Urgent (Tier 1): Biohazard/chemistry lab specimens and emergency administrative docs (Strict 15-minute SLA limit).
    • Standard (Tier 2): Inter-departmental packages and student equipment transfers (45-minute SLA).
    • Bulk (Tier 3): Routine campus mail and recycled consumables (2-hour SLA window).
  3. Multi-Compartment Dynamic Batching: Each rover is equipped with 3 lockable modular cargo bins. The system decides whether to dispatch a rover immediately with a single urgent item or hold for 3–5 minutes to batch co-located packages heading to adjacent campus buildings (e.g., Central Receiving → Science Complex → Student Center).
  4. Dispatching & Resource Contention: Allocates ready rovers from the active fleet. If all rovers are in transit or recharging, requests accumulate in building holding queues, triggering SLA violation penalties.
  5. Pedestrian-Interactive Transit & Bottlenecks: Rovers navigate outdoors between campus buildings. Walkway transit times are not static delays; they fluctuate dynamically based on the 10-minute passing periods between classes, where pedestrian density surges by up to 500%, forcing rovers to decelerate, detour, or halt.
  6. Delivery Handshake & Unsuccessful Delivery Subsystem: Upon arriving at a destination hub, the recipient is alerted. If the recipient does not claim the package within a 3-minute window:
    • Primary Fallback: Deposit package into a secure automated campus parcel locker (if slots are available).
    • Secondary Fallback: Abort delivery, flag as "Delivery Exception", retain package in rover cargo bin, and schedule a return trip to Central Receiving, directly restricting cargo capacity.
  7. Energy Replenishment & Depot Management: Rovers monitor State-of-Charge (SOC). When entering charging depot (Station D), rovers contend for limited inductive charging slots (2 pads for 3–5 rovers). The system balances fast-charging downtime against peak dispatch availability.

1.4 Reinforcement Learning: Sequential Multi-Objective Decision Making

The RL supervisory agent does not merely find the shortest path; it solves a complex, sequential Multi-Objective Markov Decision Process (MOMDP) coordinating batching, assignment, and preemptive energy management:

State Space Vector St ∈ ℝ10 (Observed Every Decision Step):
  1. [Q_HubA, Q_HubB, Q_HubC]: Queue depths at Central Mail, Science Lab, and Admin Hub.
  2. [U_HubA, U_HubB, U_HubC]: Number of urgent (Tier 1) parcels awaiting immediate pickup.
  3. [SOC_R1, SOC_R2, SOC_R3]: Battery percentage (0.0–1.0) of each rover in the fleet.
  4. Ped_Density: Real-time pedestrian walkway congestion index (0.2 calm to 1.0 passing rush).
  5. Docks_Free: Number of available charging pads at Station D (0, 1, or 2).
Action Space At (Discrete Combinatorial Decisions):
  • Action 0..2: Immediate Express Dispatch: Assign available rover to Hub A, B, or C for single urgent delivery.
  • Action 3..5: Batch & Route: Instruct rover to load 2–3 co-located parcels across adjacent buildings before departure.
  • Action 6: Opportunistic Top-off Charge: Pull a moderately charged rover (e.g. 55% SOC) into Station D for a brief top-off before the upcoming class rush hour.
  • Action 7: Congestion-Bypass Route: Route rover via secondary perimeter pathway to avoid high-density pedestrian courtyards.
  • Action 8: Standby: Hold rover at strategic central node to conserve battery.
Multi-Objective Reward Function (Rt):
Rt = + 15 × Ndelivered - 0.5 × ∑ WaitTimes - 25 × ∑ SLA_Violations - 0.8 × EnergyConsumed - 100 × I(Rover Stranded)

Comparing Q-Learning vs. PPO: Tabular/DQN Q-learning handles discrete assignment states well in low-traffic conditions, but PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) excels at continuous policy smoothing—anticipating pedestrian crowd surges and scheduling opportunistic top-off charging prior to class breaks, preventing deadlocks and SLA violations.
