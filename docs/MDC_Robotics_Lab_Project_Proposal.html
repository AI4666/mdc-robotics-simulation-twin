1. Executive Summary & Problem Context

Real-World Problem Setting: Miami Dade College AI & Robotics Lab

In the Miami Dade College AI & Robotics Lab, physical equipment, shared component bins (Station A), soldering/prototyping workbenches (Station B), and specialized testing benches (Station C) experience acute bottlenecks during class labs and peak project hours. Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs) tasked with delivering parts, clearing workstations, and assisting students encounter conflicting priorities, queue congestion, and battery depletion.

Core Objectives:

  • Minimize Student Wait Times: Cut transit and queue delays for requested lab components and bench testing slots.
  • Optimize AMR Fleet Productivity: Maintain high throughput while guaranteeing robots recharge (Station D) before critical battery failure.
  • Benchmark AI Policies: Compare heuristic baselines (FIFO / threshold) against Q-Learning and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
  • Interactive Stakeholder Decision Support: Deliver insights via an interactive Streamlit Dashboard and an AnyLogic .alp model featuring full 3D animation.

4. Required AnyLogic Simulation Experiments

Experiment Purpose & Methodology Target Output
Optimization Find optimal fleet size (2 vs 3 vs 5 AMRs) and charging pads under lab budget constraints. Pareto frontier of min wait time vs. capital cost.
Compare Runs Direct head-to-head comparison between FIFO baseline, Q-Learning, and PPO policies. Comparative throughput and wait-time bar charts.
Monte Carlo 100+ replications with stochastic student arrival intervals and task durations. 95% confidence interval distributions of student wait times.
Sensitivity Analysis Vary battery drain rate (aging batteries) and student influx (±30%). Spider charts identifying tipping points for lab gridlock.
Calibration Tune simulated robot velocities and service times against physical MDC lab stopwatch data. Pre- vs. Post-calibration curve alignment.
